Training tools weren't built for people doing more than one thing.

Nothing holds it together

Every existing training app is built for one sport at a time. Strava logs and socializes. WHOOP scores recovery. TriDot plans triathlon. Runna plans running. None of them see across disciplines, so athletes are left mentally merging separate plans themselves, and when that merging fails, the result is wasted training or real injury.

Repeated finding

Two separate interview participants criticized the same competitor for prescribing running volume with no regard for the other training they were doing.

Real injury

One participant's hamstring injury was traced directly to stacked volume across running, football, and gym work, with no single view accounting for the overlap.

Unanswered question

Multiple participants raised, unprompted, the same problem: what do you prioritize when two sessions compete for the same slot?

4 interviews. 6 competitors tested. One gap nobody has closed.

I ran semi-structured interviews with hybrid athletes and one industry perspective, alongside hands-on testing of six existing apps: Strava, WHOOP, TriDot, Runna, Final Surge, and TrainingPeaks.

Meet Lina

The self-coached hybrid athlete

Late 20s to 30s, training multiple disciplines at once, comfortable with AI but unwilling to trust it blindly.

Lina plans her own training, has been injured before by unmanaged volume, and has never used a tool that sees her whole week, only ever one discipline at a time.

"You just can't do everything at once — you really have to embrace your body and be like, holy crap, I can't believe my body can achieve this."

Design decisions worth explaining

One structural pattern, five disciplines

Run, Bike, and Swim all share a single Steady/Intervals structure. Only units change. Strength and HYROX/WOD share a row-based builder with rounds and superset grouping. Five sports, one underlying system.

Conflict-flagging, built on evidence

Detection uses specific body-area tags and thresholds that adapt to the individual: recovery capacity, injury history, even current injuries. Exercises the system can't confidently read are never flagged, on principle.

An AI that admits uncertainty

The Prioritization Assistant treats physical and mental state as genuinely separate signals, reasons about recent training frequency rather than a fixed rule, and always closes by deferring to the athlete's own judgment.

Linking, not new session types

Brick sessions and supersets reuse the same lightweight linking mechanic: two independent sessions marked as intentionally back-to-back, rather than a rigid new data structure for every combination.

How it was built

  • Research and design in FigmaInterviews, a competitive audit, and every core screen designed before a line of code was written.
  • Built with Claude CodeThe full app (React, TypeScript, Tailwind) built through iterative, reviewed prompts rather than hand-written line by line.
  • AI reasoning via the Claude APIThe Prioritization Assistant calls Claude through a serverless function, keeping the API key server-side and never exposed to the browser.
  • Deployed live on VercelA real, public, working app, not a prototype.

Testing & what's next

Testing with real hybrid athletes is ongoing. One early round of feedback came from a more strategic angle, and it sharpened how I'd position and scope this going forward.

"What's the differentiation of this app from other training apps? Think TrainingPeaks, MapMyRun, Zwift, Garmin, Peloton, Nike+." Early reviewer feedback
Closest real competitor

TrainingPeaks already supports multi-sport plans, but plans each discipline separately, with no cross-discipline conflict detection and no day-to-day prioritization reasoning. That gap is still open.

Monetization

Undecided at this stage, deliberately. A sensible direction: core logging and conflict-flagging free, with the AI prioritization feature behind a light subscription, given its real per-use API cost.

Device integrations

No HRM, smart trainer, or smart scale sync yet, same reasoning as the deferred Strava integration. Real technical lift, not where the core value currently sits.

Also deliberately left out of this version, and worth naming honestly: a coach-facing view for managing multiple athletes, per-discipline recovery ratings, real push notifications, video technique guides, and drag-and-drop session rescheduling. Each is a considered next step, not an oversight.
